# Data extraction of concentrations of trace elements in marine sediment

## Introduction

The following information was applied to the process of data extraction from a systematic review of the literature in relation to concentrations of trace elements in marine sediment. The period covered was the last 15 years. The data that EFSA have collected is heterogeneous in terms of time of sample collection, method of analysis, characteristics of the sediment…and from this heterogeneous data EFSA will extract a 90<sup>th</sup> percentile to use it as natural background concentration for each trace element. What EFSA expect from the FHF data is i) to cover EFSA data gaps as much as possible; and ii) EFSA would use field experimental data to validate the results obtained from the data extracted from the systematic review.

## Data needs

There is a document with maps indicating the data points in the different marine regions per trace element. There is a serious lack of data of concentrations of selenium and iodine in marine sediment. But the spirit is “the more data we receive, the better”. In general, concentrations of all seven trace elements in all marine regions are of interest.

We think the following information, although initially tailored for the data extraction of the systematic review, may give you some orientation on the type of data EFSA needs.

**RefId**: each sample point should have a unique reference identification number.

**Notes**: open field for any information considered relevant for the data

**Pristine**: It is difficult to determine where to trace the line between pristine and non-pristine areas. And a sampling area can be considered pristine for some trace elements and non-pristine (polluted) for other trace elements. Sometimes pollution indexes (e.g., enrichment factors, EF) are calculated to allow to discriminate polluted from non-polluted samples. The best index, no matter if the area is pristine or not, would be the calculation of EF based on local background concentrations. Where EF threshold is not defined by the authors, the data having an EF\<1 are considered adequate. The Geo-accumulation Index (Igeo) and the Pollution Load Index (PLI) can be considered as well in absence of EF. Only background concentration indices can be considered. There are concerns from the consideration of the enrichment factor (EF) when values from Turekian and Wedepohl (1961) and Whitehead et al. (1985) are used as background concentrations because the result may be a distorted picture of the real background concentrations in marine sediment. Risk assessment indexes must be avoided (e.g. EQS…)

- Samples considered in principle as non-pristine: samples collected in ports, or close to discharges from rivers, close to mining, close to industrialised or very populated/turistic areas.

- Samples considered pristine: samples collected from natural marine reserves or natural protected areas. Offshore collected samples (it is not possible to set a default distance from the coastline from which to consider all samples non-polluted and it must be considered case-by-case). Samples collected in areas naturally affected by volcano emissions.

- Samples taken during / for Environmental / Ecological Impact Assessment for marine aquaculture facilities, before operation, and if available. also monitoring data of sediment quality over time (only trace elements) under the sea cages (for the validation of model under development)

Samples collected on the beach, or from lagoons, are not considered adequate.

**Date of collection**: at least the year is required. For sampling points sampled repeatedly along time, the complete date is required.

**Place of collection**: drop-down menu with the different marine regions considered relevant.

**Sample location**: in case there is information on coordinates (useful to differentiate data points), or the name of the municipality.

**Depth of sediment sample**: ordinal variable of three ranges: 0 – 5 cm, 5 – 40 cm, \>40 cm (drop-down menu). When a sample has been analysed at different depths (e.g., a sample consisting of a core), the upper layer (upper 5 cm) is the one that matches better with the scenario to calculate PEC marine sediment described in the [FEEDAP Guidance Document 2019](https://efsa.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2019.5648) on the ERA of feed additives. It was decided to consider the upper layer (0 – 5 cm) and if not available, the top 10 or 20 cm. Average of several replicates of the same core is considered a valid value. It was also noted, however, that the uppermost recent sediment may be also the most polluted. When the top layer of the core is considered polluted, the deeper core concentrations can be taken as representative of pristine area of former times. In the case of deep cores, to determine a concentration not affected by human activities, the concentration showing low variability across the length of the core should be taken as representative of the sample.

**Trace element**: trace element analysed. You may decide to dedicate an Excel spread sheet to each trace element.

**Measured value**: figure of the analytical value, without units

**SD**: in case of average calculations with \>5 measurements of the same sample

**Unit**: Unit of measurement

**Converted values into mg/kg sediment dry weight**: in case the unit of measurement is not mg/kg sediment dry weight, in this column the measured value is converted to this unit.

**Extraction class**: has three different categories:

1.  Strong- Aqua regia or strong acid digestion: Includes methods using aqua regia or combinations of HCl, HF, HNO₃, and/or HClO₄, typically aimed at total metal recovery. e.g. HF, HNO₃; HCl, HNO₃; HCl, HF, HNO₃; HClO₄, HF, HNO₃; HClO₄, HCl, HF, HNO₃; Aqua regia; Ultra clave extraction with HNO₃ and water

2.  Milder- Nitric acid and hydrogen peroxide: Milder oxidative extractions using HNO₃ alone or with H₂O₂ (and sometimes HClO₄), targeting labile, exchangeable, or organically-bound metals. e.g. HClO₄, HNO₃, H₂O₂; HClO₄, HNO₃; HNO₃; HNO₃, H₂O₂

3.  Weak or none- Other or poorly defined methods: Includes non-extraction cases, insufficiently reported procedures, or unclear protocols. e.g. Poorly reported or not disclosed; No extraction (e.g., ref. 8329 Zn)

**Analytical method**: drop down menu where you can choose. One option is “other”. In this case indicate the method under the column “notes”.

Regarding the methods of analysis, it is assumed that in the period of the literature review (last 15 years) the analytical methods are sound and reliable, and that they may have little influence on the measured value if applied properly. In principle, they will not be taken into account when calculating the 90<sup>th</sup> percentile.

**LOD**: limit of detection of the method. If instead there is only available the limit of quantification (LOQ), report this one and indicate it the column “notes”.

**Unit of LOD**: units of the limit of detection (or quantification in its absence)

**Accredited lab**: No performance requirements are set. Some data originate from national reference laboratories, which are accredited. Other publications indicate that the laboratory is following a quality control/quality assurance procedure, including reference samples and controlling the accuracy of the methods. These labs should also be considered as “accredited lab”. When there is no mention the quality control/assurance of the analyses, then the quality of the overall publication should be checked. If the quality seems dubious, we should reject it.

**pH of porewater**: in case this information is available

**Sieve \<63 µm**: This refers to the method of preparation of the sample before the analysis. It is important to differentiate weather the analyses had been done in bulk sample, or preferably and typically in fraction \<63 µm after sieving. In case it was not sieved, it is assumed that bulk analysis was performed. Mesh filtering with pore sizes of 2mm or 1 mm is also considered as “bulk analysis.” Filtering at \<20 µm is considered as “sieve \<63 µm. It is recognized that in some cases it is not possible to know it because the paper refers to a standard operation procedure that is not available.

**Bulk analysis**: if the sample has not been sieved at \<63 µm before the analysis. If not reported, there is also this option in the drop-down menu.

**Organic matter content**: Total Organic Carbon (TOC%) percentage. In cases where data on the Organic Matter (OM%) is reported, the TOC% will be calculated (reporting it in the “Notes” column), by applying the following formula: TOC% = OM%/1.72. Based on a quick search, it was noticed that the 1.72 factor is applicable to soil. Because there is not substantial information on this matter, it was decided to use the same factor for sediment as well and a note will be included in the opinion on this aspect.

The dataset may have been published in scientific papers. In this case, the data should be included in the dataset only once. If published, report in the “Notes” column the reference of the publication.

**Confidentiality of the data**: if you wish to keep some data ask confidential, it should be indicated.
