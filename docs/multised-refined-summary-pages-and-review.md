# multised (refined): Review of the Background and Enrichment Methods, and Instructions for Two Summary Pages

*Prepared from a full read of https://seafood-hazards.github.io/multised-refined/ (all Background and Enrichment pages), August 2026.*

This document has three parts. Part 1 summarises the methods as the site presents them, so the summary pages can be written from an agreed reading. Part 2 gives step-by-step instructions for building a **Background Summary** page and an **Enrichment Summary** page (instructions only; the pages themselves are not created here). Part 3 lists methodological flaws and weaknesses found during the review, distinguishing those the site already acknowledges from those it does not.

---

## Part 1 — The methods as documented

### Context

The refined database holds seven target elements (Co, Cu, I, Mn, Mo, Se, Zn) in a single `measurement` table, split by fraction into bulk, sieved <63 µm and sieved <20 µm (never pooled), with the Fe/Al/organic-carbon ratios baked in, a `normaliser` table, a Norwegian `aquaculture` reference linked via `site.aqua_id`, and repeat-sampling grouping on sites. Iodine is too sparse for any of the analyses; selenium is sparse in the sieved fractions.

### The Background section (four estimates plus a map)

**Global Background.** Percentiles of the raw concentration (mg/kg) per element and fraction, with the 90th percentile taken as the EFSA-style background value, computed (a) over all sites and (b) restricted to offshore sites more than 10 km from the coast. Distributional outliers are dropped; groups need at least 30 values. The offshore/global P90 ratio is read as the coastal signal: copper (≈0.4) and zinc (≈0.6) drop sharply offshore in bulk; manganese and cobalt barely move; molybdenum drops steeply in bulk (P90 ≈18 → ≈3, attributed to coastal anoxic basins) but rises offshore in the sieved fractions. A sensitivity table compares 10/20/50 km cutoffs.

**Grain-Size-Normalised Background.** The same offshore-versus-global comparison after dividing the metal by a grain-size proxy, three ways: metal/Al, metal/Fe, and metal/mud (`value / (fines/100)`, only where mud ≥ 10%). For copper, Al-normalising lifts the offshore/global shift from 0.40 to 0.87, i.e. most of the apparent coastal enrichment was grain size; but the three bases disagree sharply (for copper, Al says 0.87, mud says 0.15). Al bulk coverage is only about a third of samples.

**Distribution-Mixture Background.** A two-component Gaussian mixture fitted to log10 of the raw concentration per element and fraction; the crossover between the background and enriched components is the background threshold. Manganese, molybdenum and selenium show a genuinely distinct enriched population; copper, zinc and cobalt are closer to a single continuum, so their thresholds are soft cuts. The mixture threshold is cross-checked against the offshore P90 (independent information), and the two agree reasonably for copper (≈28 vs ≈34) and molybdenum (≈2.4 vs ≈2.9).

**Pressure-Based Background.** Norway only. Concentration summarised in distance-to-nearest-farm bands (<1, 1–5, 5–20, >20 km); the >20 km band is the background, and the near/far ratio of the P90s is the near-cage enrichment: Mo ≈13×, Se ≈4.6×, Cu ≈3.2×, Zn ≈1.8×, Co ≈1.4×, Mn 0.47× (below 1, read as diagenetic rather than pressure-driven). Because it uses the aquaculture positions, this gradient is deliberately held out of the background/pristine estimation and reserved for evaluation.

**Background Map.** All sites in grey with three overlays per element and fraction: offshore (>10 km), below offshore P90, below mixture threshold. Values are averaged per site for display; the per-measurement tables are authoritative.

### The Enrichment section (two verdicts plus a map)

**Enrichment Factor.** EF = (metal/Al)_sample ÷ (metal/Al)_background, where the background is the offshore (>10 km) **median** of metal/Al per element and fraction — a local, data-driven reference, explicitly avoiding literature crustal values per EFSA guidance. EF < 1 is read as "adequate"; classes 1–2, 2–5, >5 mark rising enrichment relative to this local reference (so not comparable to crustal-referenced literature classes). Roughly half of Co, Cu, Mn, Zn bulk samples come out EF < 1. The aquaculture gradient is then used as an evaluation: median EF by distance to farm shows Cu (>5 at the cages), Se and Mo (≈4–5), Zn (≈2.5) staying enriched after grain-size control, while Co and Mn do not. Notably, normalisation reverses the raw-value picture for copper, which was *lower* at the cages in raw mg/kg (sandier sites) but is the most enriched per Al.

**Pristine Classification.** A per-measurement, per-element verdict combining three aquaculture-free criteria: EF < 1, below the mixture threshold, and below the offshore P90. Two flags: the **EF rule** (EF < 1 alone, permissive) and the **strict rule** (all three agree). A raw-concentration-only version was tried and rejected because it validated backwards (sandy near-cage sediment slips under concentration thresholds). Where aluminium is absent, samples are left **unclassified**. The headline limitation: classifiability collapses near the cages — only ≈2% of samples within 1 km of a farm carry aluminium, versus ≈90% beyond 20 km — and only ≈24–26% of Cu/Zn bulk samples are classifiable at all. Validation shows the pristine share falling toward the coast and the cages among classifiable samples.

**Enrichment Map.** All sites in grey with pristine (EF), pristine (strict, drawn inside the EF ring) and unclassifiable (no Al, off by default) overlays; same per-site averaging caveat as the background map.

## Part 2 — Instructions for the two summary pages

Both pages should be Quarto documents (the site is built with Quarto 1.10) added to the site navigation as the final item of their respective menus: `background-summary.qmd` under **Background**, `enrichment-summary.qmd` under **Enrichment**. They are *synthesis* pages: they must not recompute anything, only assemble numbers already produced by the analysis pages, so that the summaries can never disagree with their sources. The map pages reveal that the analysis results already exist as machine-readable summary files under `data/refined_summary/` — at least `refined_background_compare.csv` (offshore P90s, keyed by `symbol` and `cat`), `refined_mixture_components.csv` (mixture thresholds), and `refined_ef_background.csv` (EF backgrounds, `bg_ratio_al`). Build both pages from these files (plus whichever sibling files hold the grain-size-normalised and pressure-band summaries), never from hard-coded numbers, so the summaries update automatically when the pipeline re-runs.

### Page 1: Background Summary (`background-summary.qmd`)

Purpose: one page that answers "what is the background value for element X in fraction Y, and how much should I trust it?" by placing the four estimates side by side.

1. **Header and framing.** Title "Background Summary"; subtitle along the lines of "the four background estimates side by side, per element and fraction". Open with a short paragraph stating that no single definition of background is privileged and that agreement across independent definitions is the evidence.
2. **The master table.** One row per element × fraction (bulk, sieved63, sieved20; only reliable groups, n ≥ 30, matching the analysis pages). Columns: n; global P90; offshore P90 (>10 km); mixture threshold; mixture verdict (two populations / continuum); Al-normalised background (metal/Al P90); and the Norwegian >20 km band value where it exists. Read the values from the summary CSVs. Flag with a footnote which cells are absent and why (no Al, too sparse, Norway-only).
3. **A convergence indicator.** Add a derived column or small figure showing the spread of the estimates that share units (global P90, offshore P90, mixture threshold, >20 km band are all mg/kg): for example the ratio of the maximum to the minimum of the available estimates. Where the ratio is small (copper ≈ 23–34, molybdenum ≈ 2.4–3.7 in bulk), say the background is well-constrained; where it is large (molybdenum's global P90 of ≈18 versus everything else at ≈2–4), say the global figure is not a usable background and explain in one sentence why (coastal anoxic basins).
4. **A per-element verdict paragraph.** Six short paragraphs (Co, Cu, Mn, Mo, Se, Zn; iodine gets one sentence: too sparse). Each names the recommended background value and definition for that element, e.g.: copper — offshore P90 ≈ 23–34 mg/kg bulk, corroborated by the mixture threshold, but note most of its coastal signal is grain size; manganese — do not use the offshore filter as a cleaner value (diagenetic, offshore P90 exceeds global at wider cutoffs); molybdenum — bulk global values unusable, offshore ≈3 mg/kg with the anoxic-basin caveat.
5. **A caveat box.** Carry forward, verbatim in spirit, the three cross-cutting caveats: "offshore" is a coarse proxy for pristine; the P90 is a conservative upper bound, not a typical value; the three grain-size normalisers disagree and cover different samples. Add any caveats adopted from Part 3 below (at minimum: sampling is programme-driven, not spatially representative, so percentiles are percentiles of the monitoring effort, not of the seabed).
6. **Link discipline.** Every number's cell or paragraph links back to the page it came from. End with the standard note that the pressure-based page is Norway-only and held out for evaluation.

### Page 2: Enrichment Summary (`enrichment-summary.qmd`)

Purpose: one page that answers "how enriched is the collection, how much of it can be judged at all, and does the verdict hold up against the known pressure?"

1. **Header and framing.** Title "Enrichment Summary". Open by stating the design in two sentences: the verdicts (EF, pristine) use no aquaculture information; the aquaculture gradient is the held-out yardstick.
2. **The verdict table.** One row per element (bulk; add fractions if space allows). Columns: samples; % classifiable (has Al); % pristine under the EF rule; % pristine under the strict rule; median EF; P90 EF; near-cage enrichment from the pressure page (near/far P90 ratio); and a one-word evaluation verdict ("confirmed" where median EF rises toward the cages — Cu, Se, Mo, Zn; "no signal" for Co, Mn). Source all numbers from the summary CSVs / the EF and pristine tables.
3. **The coverage figure.** Reproduce (by inclusion, not recomputation) or link the classifiability-by-distance figure, and state the two headline numbers in text: ≈2% classifiable within 1 km of a farm, ≈90% beyond 20 km. This is the single most decision-relevant fact in the whole Enrichment section and should be impossible to miss.
4. **What the classification can and cannot say.** Two short paragraphs: (a) open-shelf data — trustworthy, grain-size-controlled, cross-validated against the pressure gradient; (b) near-cage data — largely unassessable, with the concrete recommendation repeated: aluminium (or another normaliser) must be reported alongside trace elements at aquaculture sites.
5. **Rule choice guidance.** A short paragraph telling a reader which flag to use: the EF rule for EFSA-style reporting (permissive, single grain-size-controlled criterion), the strict rule where a conservative subset is needed — with the caveat that the strict rule inherits the mixture's unreliability for continuum elements (cobalt's strict share of 20% vs 56% under EF is an artefact of that, not evidence of enrichment).
6. **Caveat box.** EF is relative to the offshore median, so "EF < 1" is not an absolute verdict; classes are not comparable to crustal-referenced literature; Al coverage bias means the classifiable subset is not a random sample. Add adopted items from Part 3 (at minimum: the median-denominator issue and the strict rule's internal inconsistency, both below).
7. **Cross-links.** Link the EF, Pristine and Enrichment Map pages, and the Pressure-Based Background page as the evaluation source.

### Shared implementation notes

Register both pages in `_quarto.yml` navigation. Follow the site's existing conventions: figure captions in italics, a closing "Note" callout, tables via the same table renderer as the analysis pages, and the reliable-groups rule (n ≥ 30) applied consistently. If a number needed by a summary is not yet in a `refined_summary` CSV (e.g. the pressure-band ratios), extend the pipeline to emit it rather than typing it into the page.

## Part 3 — Flaws and weaknesses found in the analyses

The site is unusually candid — each page ends with an honest caveat note, the backwards-validating raw-concentration flag was tried and rejected, and the aquaculture hold-out design is genuinely good practice. The points below therefore separate what is already acknowledged from what is not.

### Acknowledged by the site (listed for completeness)

Offshore distance is a coarse proxy for pristine; the P90 is an upper bound, not a typical background; the three grain-size normalisers disagree and cover different sample subsets; the mixture is fitted on raw values so grain size can fake a second population; the pressure gradient is Norway-only, non-monotonic, and partly grain-size-driven; EF depends entirely on its background choice; aluminium covers only about a third of bulk samples; near-cage samples are almost never classifiable.

### Not acknowledged, or understated

**1. The EF "adequate" threshold is at war with its median denominator.** The EF background is the offshore *median* of metal/Al, and the verdict is EF < 1. By construction, about half of the offshore reference population itself — the cleanest sediment the dataset has — fails EF < 1. The observed ≈44–64% "adequate" shares hover around 50% largely because of this arithmetic, not because half the sea floor is enriched. Either the reference should be a higher percentile of the offshore distribution (e.g. P90 of metal/Al, matching the concentration pages), or the adequate threshold should be EF < ~1.5–2 (the conventional "no/minimal enrichment" band). As it stands, the pristine EF rule misclassifies roughly half of genuinely background sediment as non-pristine, which silently deflates every "% pristine" figure downstream, including in the validation plots.

**2. The strict rule contradicts the page's own stated principle.** The Pristine page states that "only grain-size-controlled verdicts are trusted" and that a raw-concentration flag was rejected for validating backwards — yet two of the strict rule's three criteria (below the mixture threshold, below the offshore P90) are raw-concentration criteria. ANDing them onto EF < 1 cannot make a sample falsely pristine, but it does mean the strict rule re-imports exactly the grain-size confound the page disavows: muddy but clean samples fail the raw criteria and are stripped of their pristine flag (cobalt's 56% → 20% collapse is partly this, not only the mixture's unreliability). The strict rule should either use grain-size-controlled versions of all three criteria or be presented as "EF plus raw-value screens" rather than as three peers.

**3. The three "independent" criteria are not independent, and the cross-check is partly circular.** The mixture threshold and the offshore P90 are both statistics of the same concentration distribution (the offshore subset is a large chunk of the global sample). Their agreement is presented as "a real cross-check" from "unrelated information", but the overlap in data means partial agreement is built in. The genuinely independent axis is location vs distribution *shape*; the summary pages should describe the check as suggestive, not as independent confirmation.

**4. Percentiles of a convenience sample are treated as percentiles of the seabed.** The database merges five monitoring programmes whose sampling is driven by regulation and concern — harbours, fjords, impact studies are oversampled relative to open shelf. A global P90 over such data is a P90 of monitoring effort, not of marine sediment, and it will drift whenever a source is added. The repeat-sampling grouping exists in the database (`repeat_group`, `n_years`) but the percentile tables appear to be computed per measurement, so heavily re-sampled sites (typically the monitored, i.e. pressured, ones) are over-weighted — pseudo-replication that inflates the enriched tail. At minimum the backgrounds should be computed on per-site (or per-site-year) aggregates; the maps already average per site, so the site currently uses two different weightings in different places without saying so.

**5. Outlier removal is circular with respect to the background.** "Distributional outliers are dropped" before computing P90s and fitting mixtures, but the enriched tail is precisely what the outlier filter trims. Depending on how the flag was set (on the same distributions), the background thresholds and especially the mixture's enriched component are conditioned on a prior decision about what is too high — the analysis partially assumes its answer. The criterion should be stated on the background pages and its sensitivity shown.

**6. Cross-source analytical comparability is unexamined where it matters most: aluminium.** The seven elements come from five sources with different labs and, almost certainly, different digestion methods. Aluminium is notoriously digestion-sensitive (partial HNO₃ digests recover a fraction of total Al; HF recovers all of it), so metal/Al ratios — and therefore every EF and every pristine verdict — are only comparable across sources if digestion is harmonised or corrected. The `method` table exists in the schema, but no Background/Enrichment page stratifies or checks EF by source or method. A source-wise EF comparison should be added before the EF-based backgrounds are trusted; the same concern applies more mildly to Fe.

**7. Ratio normalisation itself is the weakest defensible choice.** Dividing metal by Al assumes a proportional relationship through the origin. Sediment geochemistry generally shows non-zero intercepts, so simple ratios over-correct sandy samples and under-correct muddy ones (spurious-correlation literature, e.g. the standard critiques of ratio normalisation). A regression-based normalisation (metal on Al, background as residual band) is the stronger standard approach and would also make the "EF < 1" cut less arbitrary. Relatedly, applying Al-normalisation within sieved fractions double-controls grain size, which may explain some odd sieved EF backgrounds (e.g. Mo sieved metal/Al far below bulk).

**8. The claimed cutoff insensitivity is contradicted by its own table.** The Global Background page says sensitivity to the 10/20/50 km cutoff "is small for the strong signals", but the table shows copper's offshore P90 falling 34 → 23 → 22 and zinc's 130 → 87 → 77 between 10 and 50 km — a 30–40% change in the headline background values. Ten kilometres is evidently not far enough out for Cu and Zn; either the >20 km cutoff should be the reference or the text should stop calling this small. Downstream, the EF background uses the >10 km subset, so every copper and zinc EF is anchored to the cutoff most contaminated by residual coastal signal.

**9. Two mixture-model technicalities.** The number of components is fixed at two and never tested (BIC/bootstrap against 1 or 3 components); for the "continuum" elements a one-component fit may be preferred, in which case the crossover threshold is an artefact of forcing k = 2 (cobalt, with a threshold of 6.45 sitting almost on the background geometric mean of 6.22 and only 31% assigned to "background", looks exactly like this failure mode — a minority "background" component contradicts the method's own premise that enrichment is the minority). And multimodality in a pan-European pool can reflect geological provinces or source-programme mixtures as much as contamination; fitting per region would separate these.

**10. Confounded validation, in both directions.** The pristine validation ("pristine share falls toward the cages") is computed on classifiable samples only, but classifiability itself collapses toward the cages (≈2% within 1 km) and the near-cage classifiable few are a peculiar subset (the rare programmes that measured Al there). The trend is therefore driven by mid-and-far distances and by programme composition, not cleanly by pressure. Conversely, the pressure gradient used as the yardstick is itself confounded: Norwegian farms sit preferentially in sheltered, organic-rich, often periodically anoxic fjords, which enrich Mo (and modulate Se) for natural redox reasons — so part of the ≈13× Mo "near-cage enrichment" may be fjord geochemistry co-located with farms rather than farm input. A comparison of near-cage sites against *matched* fjord sites without farms (rather than against the >20 km pool) would separate these. Temporal alignment is also unstated: if sediment samples predate the linked farm (or postdate a closed one — the aquaculture table includes closed sites), distance-to-farm is not the pressure the sediment experienced.

**11. Smaller reporting issues.** Non-detects/LOD handling is never mentioned, though Se and Mo sit near typical detection limits and censoring treatment shifts low percentiles and mixture fits. The map pages average values per site across all years and depths, so a site that was clean in 1990 and enriched in 2020 appears as one blended dot (the pages do flag that tables are authoritative, but the strict-rule map applies thresholds to these averages, which is a different classifier than the per-measurement one). And the `enrichment-map` strict classifier compares `avg_value` without the null guard the other criteria use — harmless while `avg_value` is never null, but fragile.

### What to carry into the summary pages

Items 1, 2, 4 and 8 change headline numbers and should be stated as caveats on the summary pages themselves (Part 2, steps 5–6). Items 5, 6, 7, 9 and 10 are recommendations for further work and belong in a closing "known limitations / next steps" note. None of them undermine the two most robust findings, which the summaries can state with confidence: the near-cage enrichment of Mo, Se, Cu and Zn survives grain-size control, and the aluminium coverage gap makes near-cage sediment largely unassessable — the project's own most important conclusion.
